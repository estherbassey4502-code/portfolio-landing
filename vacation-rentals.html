Overview

Bringing the moving parts of vacation rental management into one system. — Managing vacation rentals becomes increasingly complex as a business grows. Properties, bookings, distribution channels, pricing, maintenance, guest communication and day-to-day operations all need to work together.

I worked on Gopaddi Vacation Rentals, an operating system designed to help vacation rental businesses manage these interconnected parts of their business from one platform.

My role was to translate a broad operational product into an experience that felt structured, understandable and manageable, while still supporting the complexity required by growing rental businesses.

The business context

Vacation rental businesses were operating across too many disconnected tools. — Running a vacation rental business goes far beyond receiving reservations.

Operators need to manage properties, keep availability accurate across booking channels, coordinate teams, monitor performance, handle maintenance and create a consistent experience for guests. As the number of properties grows, these responsibilities become harder to manage.

The challenge wasn't simply:

How might we help people manage vacation rentals?

It was:

How might we bring the different systems required to run a vacation rental business together without creating another complicated piece of software?

This led to the idea of Vacation Rentals OS — a connected platform built around four major areas of the business:

Operations — Managing properties, teams, inventory and the day-to-day work required to keep properties running.

Distribution — Managing how properties are distributed across channels while keeping listings and availability aligned.

Revenue & Intelligence — Helping operators understand performance, occupancy and revenue so they can make better-informed decisions.

Guest Experience — Managing the interactions that happen around a stay, from communication and access to creating a more consistent experience across properties.

The product needed to connect these areas while still allowing each one to remain easy to understand independently.

Research & discovery

Understanding the business behind the booking. — One of the biggest shifts during discovery was recognising that the booking itself is only a small part of the vacation rental ecosystem.

Behind every stay is a network of operational activities. A property needs to be available. Its listing needs to be distributed. Pricing needs to be accurate. The property needs to be ready. Guests need the right information. Access needs to be coordinated.

And once the stay ends, the operator still needs visibility into how the property performed.

This helped us map the experience as a connected ecosystem rather than a collection of unrelated features.

Mapping the product ecosystem — I broke the product into its major operational relationships:

Property → Distribution → Reservation → Operations → Guest Stay → Performance

This made it easier to identify where information needed to flow between modules and where users would need visibility across the system.

It also exposed an important UX challenge:

How do you design for a product this broad without making every screen feel complex?

That question became central to many of the decisions that followed.

Key decisions

01. Organise the product around how the business operates — Rather than exposing users to a long collection of unrelated tools, the product architecture was organised around the core areas of running a vacation rental business. This gave users clearer mental models for where different tasks belonged.

Instead of thinking “Which feature do I need?”, the experience could support questions such as:

“What's happening across my properties?”

“Where are my properties being distributed?”

“How is the business performing?”

“What's happening with my guests?”

The information architecture became a way of simplifying the product before we even reached individual screens.

02. Make the portfolio visible before making users dig into properties — For operators managing multiple properties, the product couldn't behave as though every property existed independently. Users needed an understanding of their business at a higher level first.

The experience therefore prioritised portfolio-level visibility, while still allowing operators to move into individual properties when they needed more detail.

Portfolio → Property → Detail / Action

This avoided forcing users to repeatedly navigate individual properties just to understand what was happening across the business.

03. Connect operations instead of designing isolated modules — Distribution, operations, revenue and guest experience aren't independent systems. A change in one part of the business can affect another.

Rather than designing each module as its own destination, I considered how information and actions should move between them.

The goal was for the platform to feel like one operating system, not several products sitting inside the same navigation.

04. Prioritise exceptions and actions over raw information — An operations platform can easily become a collection of dashboards filled with numbers. But operators don't only need to know what is happening. They need to know:

What requires my attention?

This influenced how information was prioritised across the experience. Instead of treating every piece of information equally, the interface could surface important states, issues and actions more prominently while allowing secondary information to remain accessible when needed.

This helped reduce the amount of information users had to process at once.

05. Treat the guest experience as part of operations — Guest experience wasn't designed as something separate from property management. Communication, property information and access all depend on operational information being accurate.

For example, the work also considered experiences around digital/self check-in and property access, allowing the guest-facing experience to connect back to how the property itself is managed.

This meant thinking about two sides of the product: What does the operator need to manage? And what does the guest need to experience?

The system needed to support both without requiring operators to manually bridge the gap between them.

06. Separate the product experience from the marketing experience — The Vacation Rentals OS itself continued to follow the broader product design system. However, when designing the Vacation Rentals landing page, I wanted the marketing experience to communicate more of the feeling associated with travel and vacation.

That's where I introduced pink alongside the existing brand colours. The distinction was intentional:

Product UI → functional, consistent and operational
Landing page → expressive, energetic and aspirational

This allowed the landing page to have its own personality without unnecessarily changing the visual language of the product itself.

What was delivered

A connected experience across the vacation rental lifecycle. — The final work brought the different areas of vacation rental management into a structured product ecosystem.

Operations — Experiences for managing the operational side of vacation rentals, giving operators visibility into their properties, teams, inventory and day-to-day activities.

The goal was to make it easier to understand what's happening across a portfolio without having to piece that information together manually.

Distribution — A central experience for managing how properties are distributed across external channels.

This created a foundation for operators to manage listings and availability across their distribution ecosystem without treating every channel as a completely separate workflow.

Revenue & Intelligence — Performance experiences designed to help operators understand how their vacation rental business is performing.

Instead of simply presenting data, the goal was to make information around occupancy, revenue and property performance easier to interpret and act on.

Guest Experience — Guest-facing and operational experiences designed around creating more consistent stays.

This included thinking through areas such as guest communication, property information and digital/self check-in, connecting what the guest experiences with what the operator manages behind the scenes.

Vacation Rentals Landing Page — I also designed the customer-facing experience used to communicate the product.

The landing page translated a broad operational system into a much simpler value proposition—showing operators how the platform connects the major parts of their business rather than overwhelming them with an exhaustive list of features.

This was also where I explored a more expressive visual direction, introducing pink as an accent alongside the existing brand language to make the experience feel warmer, more energetic and more connected to travel.

Closing

Designing the system behind the stay. — One of my biggest takeaways from Vacation Rentals was that the experience guests see is only the surface.

Behind every smooth stay is a network of properties, people, distribution channels, operational tasks, revenue decisions and guest interactions that have to work together.

Designing Vacation Rentals therefore became less about creating individual screens and more about deciding how those moving parts should connect—and how much of that complexity the user should have to manage at any given moment.

The result was an experience designed around a simple principle:

The business can be complex. The experience doesn't have to be.
